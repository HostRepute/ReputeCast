## Done so far (W01: sending providers & routing, M3)

- Adapters for Amazon SES (SESv2, SNS signature check and subscription handshake, Easy DKIM, configuration-set tracking, VDM flag, AWS status feed), Postmark (batch send, Broadcast stream, basic-auth webhooks, outbound stats as its own reputation), Postal (RS256 signed webhooks via JWKS, message backfill) and generic SMTP (go-mail, DSN/ARF parsing, IMAP and POP3 bounce mailboxes). - Routing per stream (primary, weighted split, conditions on mailbox provider/list/sender domain, fallback, failover policy), bulk-terms validation with an acknowledged override, route tester; warm-up plans with guards, slowdowns, share steps and a daily job; suppression sync per provider; provider-side tracking checks; community and generic HTTP adapters (instance admins).

Demo data matches the boards; `internal/providers/state` and `manage.UsageOf` are the contract other screens read.

## Done so far (W16: community adapter runtime and SDK)

- The adapter protocol, version 1 (`docs/adapters.md` › Community adapters): signed JSON over HTTP(S) between ReputeCast and an adapter process, with a manifest (capabilities, terms, pricing, connection form, network permissions), one operation per Provider method and optional interface, HMAC-SHA256 signatures on requests and responses with a per-installation secret (sealed, shown once, rotation with a 24-hour grace), idempotency keys derived from the batch, per-operation timeouts and size limits, and error codes that map to provider health.
- `internal/providers/remote`: providers whose method set follows the declared capabilities, a circuit breaker and a concurrency limit per installed adapter, strict answer validation, calls through the same SSRF-guarded client as provider adapters (netguard, `providers.allow_private_networks`), webhooks verified by the adapter at receipt (429 with Retry-After to the provider while it is unreachable) and parsed later with `verified: true`. Installed community adapters of both kinds are registered at startup, after installs, updates and removals (a hook in `manage`) and every 15 seconds in every process: `community:<id>` for remote adapters, `community:<name>` for installed HTTP mappings, which open through `httpgeneric.FromManifest`; code that lists `installed_adapters` rows finds their registry entry with `providers.InstalledKey`. Failures reach provider health like a built-in adapter's (Down · auth failed when the provider rejects the credentials, Down after two failed checks when the adapter process stops answering). A Go fake adapter passes the shared contract suite.
- Operators: `reputecast adapter install|set-url|secret|check|list|remove`, the runtime API for instance admins (`/adapters/{adapterId}/runtime`), `providers.allow_private_networks`, audit entries for every change. The Adapters tab's generic HTTP form gained request headers, webhook authentication and the paths of a custom JSONPath map; saving checks the mapping (`Mapping.Validate`) field by field, problems about fields the form isn't showing appear under it, and "Test with a sample message" renders the request (secrets masked).
- `sdk/adapter-sdk` (`@reputecast/adapter-sdk`, no runtime dependencies): `defineAdapter`, `serve`, `ctx.http` with the network allowlist, webhook helpers, the bounce taxonomy and the contract runner (`npx reputecast-adapter test`). `sdk/adapter-template`: the example-esp adapter from the Adapters board with its manifest, tests, Dockerfile and README; `pnpm install` in `sdk/` builds the SDK the template imports. CI runs the template as a Node process through the Go runtime against the contract suite (`make sdk` locally). Before copies of the template outside this repository can install it, `@reputecast/adapter-sdk` 0.1.0 has to be published to npm.

## Done so far (W12: sending engine, live monitor and tracking, M7)

- Sending engine (`internal/sending/engine`, `App.Sending`): starting a send reruns Send Readiness through the `sending.Readiness` seam (W11's package plugs in from its wire file; a Blocked recheck stops the send, puts a scheduled campaign back to draft and tells the author and the approvers); scheduled campaigns start on time from a River job, also for a send at each recipient's best time (their most frequent open hour within the next 24 hours). The recipient list is built in pages of 2,000 from the audience (lists and W02's segments) minus the shared suppression list and unsubscribed, bounced, complained, paused, role-address and sunset-stopped contacts, each recipient routed through W01's rules (conditions, deterministic weighted split, warm-up tiers and caps, Broadcast failover policy) and queued in a shuffled order, so the first minutes of a send are a fair sample of its audience.
- Rendering per recipient (Liquid with fallbacks over the composer's stored HTML, A/B subject, content and send-time variants with the winner rule), open pixel and click rewriting with signed tokens unless the provider keeps its own tracking, and the bulk headers (one-click List-Unsubscribe at `App.URLs` plus mailto, List-Unsubscribe-Post, Feedback-ID; Message-ID from the message id). Batches go out from River jobs within each provider's shared per-second window and each mailbox provider's throttle (workspace defaults from Settings › Providers, then Send Readiness); every message is claimed before it is handed over, retried with exponential back-off and never sent twice. Sending jobs resume within seconds after a restart.
- Guarding: counters per sending provider, mailbox provider and variant; bounce, complaint, deferral and API-error rates checked every two seconds against the campaign's thresholds (over the workspace's), tripping when a rate is over its limit with enough evidence. A trip holds only the mailbox provider or sending provider it concerns (or the campaign, by choice) and records the incident with what tripped, likely causes found in the send's own data (an import or a lapsed group, deferrals) and recommendations; resume, resume with changes (exclude the group, lower the rate, hand them to a live flow), cancel the share or the send. Outages (failing calls for three minutes, or W01's health job) hold the provider's share, ask by default and move automatically only to a warm provider when the Broadcast policy allows; retry, move with cost, time, quota and risk, or cancel. Notifications send_paused and send_finished; every action is audited, with the agent as actor for MCP keys (the Ops agent can pause, never start or resume).
- Live monitor (`/campaigns/{id}/live`): snapshot API and a server-sent event stream with heartbeats and Last-Event-ID resume; LiveMonitor at 1440 and 834, LiveMonitorMobile, the auto-paused and outage decision views (LiveMonitorPaused and its phone board, LiveMonitorOutage), connecting, lost stream with reconnection, paused by a person, complete, cancelled, failed, scheduled and not-started states, and disabled actions that say who can help. `pnpm a11y` is clean on every state.
- Tracking (`internal/tracking`): `GET /t/o/{token}` answers a no-store GIF at once and `GET /t/c/{token}` redirects only to that message's own links; writes are group-committed in the background. Human and machine opens are told apart (Apple Mail Privacy Protection, Gmail and Yahoo image proxies on arrival, scanners by agent, network and timing) and bot clicks are logged but never counted; a person opening after a proxy fetch moves the open to human in the counters and the hourly engagement. Opens, clicks and delivery outcomes of automation emails reach the automations engine.
- Automations: the "Send email" step queues through the engine on the Automations stream (automatic failover) and an outbox job sends within the providers' shared rates.
- Demo: providers marked `"demo": true` send through a simulated transport (`internal/providers/simulator`, passes the adapter contract suite) that touches no network and reports deliveries, deferrals, bounces, complaints, opens and clicks at the live boards' rates. `reputecast demo send` (development only) reproduces LiveMonitor, LiveMonitorPaused (`--scenario outlook-complaints`) and LiveMonitorOutage (`--scenario postal-outage`) with moving data; `--reset` also takes back what an earlier run changed outside the campaign. Weekly Digest #142 stays scheduled in the default demo; the sent campaigns get their send history and Weekly Digest #139 its Yahoo incident evidence.
- Measured (one process, local Postgres 18, simulator): a 50,000-recipient send hands messages over at about 11,700 per second with the provider's rate out of the way (real sends are limited by provider rates, 14 /s for SES), and about 370 per second with every simulated event applied; memory follows the per-second rate, not the audience size, and no transaction spans more than one page or one second's batch.
