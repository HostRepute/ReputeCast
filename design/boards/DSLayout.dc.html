Layout and responsive behaviour

One page at every width. Screens reflow; they never hide features on phones. The same markup serves 1440, 834 and 390; only the CSS changes, and the tablet and phone artboards on every canvas page are the proof.

Grid and spacing

Content grid

Cards sit on a fluid grid: repeat(auto-fit, minmax(300px, 1fr)) for two-up, minmax(240px) for stat tiles, minmax(380px) for the two overview columns. Columns collapse by themselves; no breakpoint-specific grids.

Page anatomy, top to bottom

1. Top bar: title, caption, tabs, actions2. Banners: pause or outage first, then warnings, then notices3. The verdict: readiness, health score or headline numbers4. The work: forms, tables, canvases5. History and secondary detail
Every screen can be read top to bottom in this order; nothing important sits below a long table.

Rules

1Never hide a feature by width. Reflow, stack, scroll inside a card; do not remove.
2The page never scrolls sideways. Anything wider than the viewport scrolls inside its own card (tables, code, flow canvases).
3Primary actions stay reachable. On phones the main action sits in the top bar or at the bottom of the form, never behind a menu.
4Targets are 44px on touch widths, 36px with a mouse. Hit areas are larger than the drawn control.
5Fixed elements earn their place. Only the phone tab bar is fixed; banners, headers and panels scroll with the page.
6Device previews stay devices. The composer's mobile preview and the live-monitor phone screen are 390px wide at every viewport; they are content, not chrome.
7One source of markup. The tablet and phone artboards on each canvas page mount the desktop screen; if a screen needs different HTML per width, the layout is wrong.
