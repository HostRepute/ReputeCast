Inside our Postgres migration: the runbook

How we moved 2.1 TB from Postgres 14 to 17 with a 41-second write pause: sizing, seeding, nine days of replication and the cutover.

SRSofia Rossi
Monday 13 October 2026 · 11 min read

On 28 September we moved Acme's main database from a self-managed Postgres 14 cluster to Postgres 17, with logical replication and a 41-second pause for writes at the cutover. Nobody outside the on-call channel noticed, which was the point.

This is the runbook we followed, step by step: how we sized the new cluster, seeded it without hammering production, kept 2.1 TB of tables in sync for nine days, and rehearsed the cutover until it bored us.

We're sharing it because every migration guide we found stopped at “then switch over”. The switching over is where the risk lives, so that's where most of this post goes.

Free preview ends here · 3 blocks

1. Size the target for the week after, not the day of

A freshly seeded replica has cold caches and no bloat, so it flatters every benchmark. We sized the new primary for the load we expected a week after the cutover, with vacuum running and caches warm: 64 vCPUs and 512 GB of memory, one size up from the old one.

2. Seed from a snapshot, then let replication catch up

Copying 2.1 TB through a publication would have held a replication slot open for most of a day. Instead we restored last night's snapshot, created the subscription with copy_data = false, and let it replay only what changed since.
